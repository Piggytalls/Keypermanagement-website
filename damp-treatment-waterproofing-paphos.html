Painting over damp hides it until spring. We find where the water is actually getting in, then stop it.

Why Cyprus properties get damp

People are often surprised that damp is a problem in a place this dry. It's precisely because of how the year splits: a long hot season, then concentrated winter rain hitting flat roofs, terraces and balconies that have spent months baking and moving in the heat.

Add a property closed up for months with no heating and no airflow, and you get the second cause — condensation. Warm damp air, cold external walls, no ventilation, and mould appears in corners, behind wardrobes and along the ceiling line without a drop of rain getting in.

The two have completely different fixes, which is why diagnosis matters more than treatment.

Diagnosis before treatment

The mistake we're most often called in to undo is treating the stain instead of the cause. Sealed and repainted, it looks solved for a season and comes back worse.

  • Establish whether it's ingress, plumbing, rising damp or condensation
  • Trace the actual entry point — often nowhere near the visible patch
  • Check roof, terrace, balcony and parapet detailing for failures
  • Rule out slow plumbing leaks behind walls and under floors
  • Photograph everything, so you can see the reasoning, not just the bill

Treating the wall properly

Once the source is dealt with, the affected structure has to be brought back — removing failed plaster where it needs removing, treating mould rather than painting over it, drying the substrate, then replastering and redecorating. Done in the right order it stays fixed; done in the wrong order you're repeating it next winter.

Waterproofing roofs, terraces and balconies

Most water ingress we see in Paphos comes through horizontal surfaces and their edges — flat roofs, terrace floors, balcony junctions and parapet details, where heat movement has opened up membranes and seals. We waterproof these properly rather than patching, because a patched membrane simply fails at the next join along.
